Audio files come in different formats depending on how they store sound data, balance audio quality, and manage file sizes.
The three most common formats are **WAV**, **FLAC**, and **MP3**.

---

### 1. WAV (Waveform Audio File Format)

- **Type:** Lossless / Uncompressed
- **How it works:** Developed by Microsoft and IBM, WAV files store raw, exact audio data without any compression. Every
  single detail of the original sound wave is preserved sample by sample.
- **Pros:** Absolute highest audio quality; perfect for professional audio editing, archiving, and steganography (as
  discussed earlier, because no data is thrown away).
- **Cons:** Extremely large file sizes, making them impractical for streaming over the internet or storing many songs on a
  phone.

---

### 2. FLAC (Free Lossless Audio Codec)

- **Type:** Lossless / Compressed
- **How it works:** FLAC reduces the file size of high-quality audio significantly (usually shrinking it to about 50-60% of a
  WAV file) without losing a single bit of audio data. It uses smart mathematical compression, similar to how a ZIP file
  shrinks data.
- **Pros:** Perfect audio quality identical to the original recording, but takes up roughly half the storage space of a WAV
  file. It is also open-source.
- **Cons:** While widely supported today, some older hardware or specific music players may not play FLAC files natively
  without conversion.

---

### 3. MP3 (MPEG-1 Audio Layer III)

- **Type:** Lossy / Compressed
- **How it works:** MP3 uses **lossy compression**, meaning it permanently deletes audio data that the average human ear
  cannot easily hear or distinguish (using a process called psychoacoustics). It discards very high and low frequencies and
  masks subtle sounds happening right next to loud sounds.
- **Pros:** Tiny file sizes (often 10x smaller than a WAV file), making them fast to download, easy to stream, and compatible
  with virtually every device on the planet.
- **Cons:** Reduced audio quality compared to the original source. Because data is permanently thrown away, you cannot
  restore an MP3 back into studio-quality sound.

---

### Quick Comparison Table

| Feature           | WAV                                            | FLAC                                  | MP3                                                     |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Compression**   | None (Uncompressed)                            | Lossless (Compressed)                 | Lossy (Compressed)                                      |
| **Audio Quality** | Maximum / Original                             | Maximum / Original                    | Reduced (Perceptually similar)                          |
| **File Size**     | Very Large                                     | Medium                                | Small                                                   |
| **Best Used For** | Professional editing, archiving, steganography | Audiophile music listening, archiving | Daily streaming, portable music players, sharing online |
