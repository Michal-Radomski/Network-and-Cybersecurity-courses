Think of a digital audio file as a long sequence of **numbers**. A _sample_ is essentially one measurement of the sound
waveform at one instant.

### 1. First: what is a sample?

Suppose we record audio at **44,100 samples per second (44.1 kHz)**.

For every second of audio, we store:

**44,100 individual measurements**

A simplified waveform might look like:

```text
Amplitude
   ↑
 +1 |          ●
    |       ●     ●
  0 |----●-----------●----●----→ time
    | ●                 ●
 -1 |
```

Each `●` represents a **sample**.

A single sample is just a number, for example:

```text
127
```

or, for signed 16-bit audio:

```text
-8234
```

It represents the amplitude of the sound at that particular sampling instant.

---

## 2. WAV: samples are stored directly

WAV is usually a container for **uncompressed PCM audio**.

For example, consider:

- Sample rate: **44,100 Hz**
- Bit depth: **16 bits**
- Channels: **2 (stereo)**

Every sample is a 16-bit number.

For the left channel you might have:

```text
Sample 0:   120
Sample 1:   850
Sample 2:  2100
Sample 3:  3900
Sample 4:  5200
Sample 5:  6100
...
```

And the right channel has its own samples:

```text
Left:   120, 850, 2100, 3900, 5200, 6100, ...
Right:  100, 700, 1900, 3500, 4900, 5900, ...
```

So one **stereo frame** consists of one left sample + one right sample:

```text
Frame 0: [Left: 120]  [Right: 100]
Frame 1: [Left: 850]  [Right: 700]
Frame 2: [Left: 2100] [Right: 1900]
...
```

At 44.1 kHz, there are **44,100 frames per second**.

---

## 3. What does a 16-bit sample actually look like?

A 16-bit PCM sample is just **16 binary bits**.

For example:

```text
00000101 11001010
```

That's one number.

With signed 16-bit PCM, the possible values are approximately:

```text
-32768 ───────── 0 ───────── +32767
             ↑
          silence
```

So:

```text
00000000 00000000
```

represents approximately zero amplitude (silence).

A positive value means the waveform is above the center line, while a negative value means it is below it.

# 4. What about MP3?

This is where things get very different.

**MP3 does not simply contain the original PCM samples.**

When you convert:

```text
WAV/PCM
   ↓
 MP3 encoder
   ↓
MP3 compressed data
```

the encoder analyzes the sound, transforms it into frequency-domain information, removes information that is considered less
perceptually important, and compresses the remaining information.

So inside an MP3 you don't simply have:

```text
120
850
2100
3900
5200
...
```

stored as individual samples.

Instead, MP3 contains **compressed frames**, which describe the audio in a much more complicated representation.

During playback:

```text
MP3 file
   ↓
MP3 decoder
   ↓
PCM samples
   ↓
DAC
   ↓
speaker
```

The decoder reconstructs PCM samples from the compressed MP3 data.

---

## 5. MP3 frames vs audio samples

This distinction is important:

**Sample ≠ frame**

For example, an MP3 frame contains information from a portion of the audio and, after decoding, produces a number of PCM
samples.

For MPEG-1 Layer III (common MP3), an MP3 frame represents:

**1152 PCM samples per channel**

So conceptually:

```text
MP3 file

┌──────────── MP3 frame ────────────┐
│ compressed audio information      │
└───────────────────────────────────┘
                 ↓ decoder
┌───────────────────────────────────┐
│ PCM sample 0                      │
│ PCM sample 1                      │
│ PCM sample 2                      │
│ ...                               │
│ PCM sample 1151                   │
└───────────────────────────────────┘
```

For stereo, those are 1152 samples **per channel**.

---

## 6. An important detail: samples aren't little pieces of sound

It's tempting to imagine this:

```text
sound
├── sample 1 = tiny piece of sound
├── sample 2 = tiny piece of sound
├── sample 3 = tiny piece of sound
└── ...
```

That's not quite correct.

A sample is a **single amplitude measurement** at a particular time.

For example, if the sampling rate is 44.1 kHz:

```text
time →

0       22.7µs      45.4µs      68.0µs
│          │           │           │
●          ●           ●           ●
120       850        2100        3900
```

The time between samples is:

**1 / 44,100 ≈ 22.68 microseconds**

The collection of those measurements forms the digital representation of the waveform.

---

### In short

```text
REAL SOUND
   ↓ microphone
analog electrical signal
   ↓ ADC
PCM samples
   ↓
WAV
```

For MP3:

```text
REAL SOUND
   ↓ microphone
analog signal
   ↓ ADC
PCM samples
   ↓ MP3 encoder
compressed MP3 frames
   ↓ decoder
PCM samples
   ↓ DAC
analog signal
   ↓
speaker
```

So **WAV/PCM is basically a sequence of amplitude numbers**, while **MP3 is a compressed representation from which those
samples are reconstructed during decoding**.
