To understand how digital images are stored and displayed, it helps to look at how individual **pixels** (picture elements)
are represented using different color models.

---

### 1. Grayscale Pixel

A grayscale image consists of shades of gray, ranging from pure black to pure white.

- **Channels:** 1 channel (Intensity/Brightness).
- **Value Range:** Usually 0 to 255 (8-bit), where `0` is pure black and `255` is pure white.

> **Example:** A medium-gray pixel.
>
> - **Value:** `128`

---

### 2. RGB Pixel (Red, Green, Blue)

An RGB image represents colors by combining light intensities of the three primary colors.

- **Channels:** 3 channels (Red, Green, Blue).
- **Value Range:** Each channel ranges from `0` to `255`.

> **Example:** A bright pure red pixel.
>
> - **Values:** `(255, 0, 0)` _(Max red, no green, no blue)_
>
> **Example:** A pure white pixel.
>
> - **Values:** `(255, 255, 255)` _(All colors at maximum intensity)_

---

### 3. RGBA Pixel (Red, Green, Blue, Alpha)

An RGBA image adds an **Alpha** channel to RGB, which controls the transparency or opacity of the pixel.

- **Channels:** 4 channels (Red, Green, Blue, Alpha).
- **Value Range:** RGB channels range from `0` to `255`, while the Alpha channel is typically represented either from `0` to
  `255` or as a decimal fraction from `0.0` to `1.0`.

> **Example:** A semi-transparent blue pixel (50% opacity).
>
> - **Values (0–255 scale):** `(0, 0, 255, 127)`
> - **Values (0.0–1.0 scale):** `(0, 0, 255, 0.5)` _(0 = fully transparent, 1 = fully opaque)_
