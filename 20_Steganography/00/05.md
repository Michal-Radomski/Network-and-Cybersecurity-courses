Understanding how digital images work requires looking at three core foundations: **Properties**, **Pixels**, and
**Channels**. These elements dictate how an image is structured, displayed, edited, and saved.

---

## 1. Image Properties

Image properties define the overall technical specifications and dimensions of a digital graphic file. Key properties
include:

- **Resolution:** Measured in **PPI** (Pixels Per Inch) for digital screens or **DPI** (Dots Per Inch) for print. It
  determines the level of detail and sharpness an image holds.
- **Dimensions (Width & Height):** The total count of pixels running horizontally and vertically (e.g., $1920 \times 1080$
  pixels).
- **Color Mode / Depth:** The framework that dictates how colors are rendered (such as RGB, CMYK, or Grayscale) and the
  amount of data stored per pixel (bit depth, like 8-bit or 16-bit).
- **File Size and Format:** The storage footprint of the image and the compression type used (e.g., JPEG, PNG, TIFF, PSD).

---

## 2. Pixels (Picture Elements)

A pixel is the smallest controllable building block of a digital raster image on a screen.

- **The Grid System:** Digital images are laid out as a vast grid of rows and columns.
- **Color Data:** Individually, a single pixel is just a tiny square of uniform color. When thousands or millions of pixels
  are viewed together at a distance, our eyes blend them to form a cohesive picture.
- **Values:** In a standard 8-bit digital image, every pixel holds numerical intensity values ranging from $0$ (total absence
  of color/black) to $255$ (maximum intensity).

---

## 3. Channels

Channels are storage containers within an image file that hold specific components of color or data. Instead of displaying
everything at once, software splits an image into separate grayscale layers called channels.

- **Color Channels:** In an **RGB** (Red, Green, Blue) color mode, the image is broken down into three primary color
  channels—plus a composite channel. Each channel is a grayscale map showing where and how intensely that specific color
  light is applied across the pixel grid.
- $0$ in a channel means no light of that color.
- $255$ means full-intensity light of that color.

- **Alpha Channels:** Aside from color, channels can store extra data like transparency, selections, or masks (allowing
  precise edits to specific areas of an image without altering the rest).
