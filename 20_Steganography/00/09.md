**ASCII** and **Binary** are two fundamental concepts in computer science that allow computers to understand, store, and
process text, numbers, and images.

---

### 1. Binary (The Computer's Language)

At the most basic hardware level, computers don't understand words, letters, or decimal numbers. They only understand
electricity—specifically, whether a circuit is turned **on** or **off**.

- **The Bit:** The smallest unit of data in a computer is a **bit** (binary digit), which can hold only one of two values:
  **`1`** (on/true) or **`0`** (off/false).
- **The Byte:** To make binary useful, bits are grouped together into sets of 8, called a **byte**. A single byte can
  represent 256 different combinations of 1s and 0s (from `00000000` to `11111111`), which is enough to represent a single
  character like a letter or symbol.

---

### 2. ASCII (Translating Text into Numbers)

**ASCII** stands for **American Standard Code for Information Interchange**. It is a character encoding standard that bridges
human-readable text and computer-readable numbers.

- **How it works:** ASCII assigns a specific decimal number (from 0 to 127) to every English letter, digit, punctuation mark,
  and control character.
- **Examples:**
- The uppercase letter **`A`** is assigned the ASCII value **65**. In binary, that is represented as `01000001`.
- The lowercase letter **`a`** is assigned the ASCII value **97** (`01100001`).
- The number **`0`** (as text) is assigned the value **48** (`00110000`).

---

### How They Work Together

When you type the word **"Cat"** on a keyboard, the computer translates it using ASCII and Binary steps:

1. **Character to ASCII:** The computer looks up each letter in the ASCII table:

- **C** = 67
- **a** = 97
- **t** = 116

2. **ASCII to Binary:** The computer converts those numbers into binary bytes:

- **67** becomes `01000011`
- **97** becomes `01100001`
- **116** becomes `01110100`

This entire sequence (`010000110110000101110100`) is what the computer actually stores and processes in its memory.
