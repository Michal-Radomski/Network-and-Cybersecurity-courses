**Audio steganography** is the technique of hiding secret information—such as a text message, an image, or another audio
file—inside an ordinary audio file, most commonly a **.wav file**, without noticeably altering the sound.

Just like LSB (Least Significant Bit) steganography in images, audio steganography exploits the limitations of human
perception, but instead of vision, it targets human hearing.

---

### Why Use .wav Files?

In steganography, **lossless formats** (like `.wav` or `.flac`) are preferred over **lossy formats** (like `.mp3`).

- **Lossless (.wav):** Stores audio data exact sample-by-sample without discarding any data. When you hide a bit of secret
  data inside the exact bits of a `.wav` file, it stays intact.
- **Lossy (.mp3):** Uses psychoacoustic compression to throw away audio frequencies that human ears supposedly cannot hear.
  If you tried to hide secret data inside an MP3, the compression algorithm would likely destroy or scramble the hidden
  message.

---

### Techniques Used in Audio Steganography

1. **Least Significant Bit (LSB) Coding:**

- Similar to images, digital audio is made up of samples (numerical values representing sound waves at a specific moment in
  time).
- By altering the absolute lowest bit of an audio sample, the resulting change in sound volume or pitch is so minuscule that
  it is completely drowned out by the actual audio or masked by human ear limitations.

2. **Phase Coding:**

- This method alters the phase of a reference audio segment rather than its amplitude. Because human ears are generally less
  sensitive to phase changes than loudness changes, a message can be encoded into the phase shifts.

3. **Spread Spectrum:**

- The secret data is spread across a wide frequency band of the audio file at a very low volume, making it blend in like
  background noise or static. It requires a specific key to extract and decode.

---

### Applications

- **Digital Rights Management (DRM):** Embedding copyright information or serial numbers into audio tracks to track illegal
  distribution.
- **Covert Communication:** Sending hidden messages across public platforms using seemingly normal sound clips.
