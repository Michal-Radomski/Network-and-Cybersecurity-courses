### What is a Captive Portal?

A **captive portal** is a web page that users of a public or guest Wi-Fi network are forced to view and interact with before
they are granted full access to the internet.

When you connect to an open or password-protected public network (such as at a coffee shop, hotel, airport, or university)
and open your web browser, the network intercepts your initial web traffic and redirects you to this login or landing page.

---

### How a Captive Portal Works

1. **Connection:** A user connects their device (smartphone, laptop, etc.) to the local Wi-Fi network.
2. **Interception:** The device receives a local IP address via DHCP, but any attempt to visit a website (like
   `http://example.com`) is intercepted by the network router or gateway.
3. **Redirection:** The gateway redirects the browser request to a specific web page managed by the network administrator—the
   captive portal.
4. **Authentication / Acceptance:** The user must complete a required action on the page before they can browse freely.
5. **Release:** Once authenticated, the router updates its firewall or access control list (ACL) to let the device's MAC/IP
   address pass through to the public internet.

---

### Common Use Cases

- **Terms of Service & Liability:** Used by businesses to force users to agree to acceptable use policies or terms and
  conditions before using their free internet.
- **Authentication:** Requiring users to log in using credentials, a voucher code, a room number (hotels), or a member ID.
- **Marketing and Data Collection:** Asking users to sign in with an email address, social media account, or complete a short
  survey in exchange for Wi-Fi access.
- **Paid Access:** Directing users to a payment gateway to purchase high-speed internet access or time-limited passes.
