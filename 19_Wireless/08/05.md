## What is Wireless Hijacking?

**Wireless Hijacking** (often referred to as Wi-Fi hijacking or a Man-in-the-Middle attack) is a type of cybersecurity attack
where an unauthorized person intercepts, controls, or monitors wireless communication between a user's device (like a
smartphone or laptop) and a wireless access point (like a Wi-Fi router).

By inserting themselves into the network traffic, the attacker gains the ability to eavesdrop on private communications,
alter data in transit, or redirect the user to malicious websites.

---

## How Wireless Hijacking Works

Attackers typically exploit inherent vulnerabilities in standard wireless protocols or use deceptive tactics to trick devices
into connecting to them. Common stages of an attack include:

- **Reconnaissance:** The attacker scans the area for active wireless networks and vulnerable access points.
- **Deauthentication (Deauth Attack):** The attacker sends forged deauthentication frames to force a target device to
  disconnect from its legitimate Wi-Fi network.
- **Rogue Access Point Setup:** Once disconnected, the device automatically tries to reconnect to a known network or a
  network with a stronger, unencrypted signal set up by the attacker (often called an **Evil Twin** attack).
- **Interception:** Once the device connects to the rogue access point, all internet traffic passes through the attacker's
  system, allowing them to capture credentials, passwords, and personal data.

---

## Common Types of Wireless Hijacking

| Attack Type                  | Description                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Evil Twin Attack**         | Setting up a fraudulent Wi-Fi hotspot with the same name (SSID) as a legitimate network to fool users into connecting.                             |
| **Man-in-the-Middle (MitM)** | Silently intercepting and relaying communication between two parties who believe they are directly communicating with each other.                  |
| **Session Hijacking**        | Stealing a user's session token or cookie after they log into a website, allowing the attacker to impersonate them without needing their password. |
| **Packet Sniffing**          | Using software to monitor and record unencrypted data packets moving across a wireless network.                                                    |

---

## Potential Risks and Consequences

- **Data Theft:** Loss of sensitive personal information, including login credentials, credit card numbers, and private
  messages.
- **Malware Distribution:** Injecting malicious software or adware into the user's web traffic.
- **Identity Theft:** Unauthorized access to personal or financial accounts managed over compromised networks.

---

## How to Protect Against Wireless Hijacking

- **Use Encrypted Connections:** Always ensure websites use **HTTPS** (look for the padlock icon in the browser address bar),
  which encrypts data even if the network itself is insecure.
- **Enable a VPN (Virtual Private Network):** A VPN encrypts all internet traffic passing through your device, making it
  unreadable to anyone intercepting the wireless signal.
- **Avoid Public Wi-Fi for Sensitive Tasks:** Refrain from accessing online banking, shopping, or entering personal passwords
  while connected to open, unencrypted public hotspots.
- **Turn Off Auto-Connect:** Configure devices not to automatically join open or unfamiliar Wi-Fi networks.
