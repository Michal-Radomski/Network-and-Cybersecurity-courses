Adding a traditional enterprise-grade **Wireless Intrusion Prevention System (WIPS)**—which actively scans the airspace,
detects rogue access points, and can automatically block unauthorized wireless connections—to a standard home router is
generally not possible out-of-the-box, because WIPS requires specialized hardware sensors and software architecture designed
for enterprise environments.

However, you can achieve a similar level of robust wireless security and monitoring for your home network through alternative
methods.

---

### Why Standard Home Routers Don't Have True WIPS

- **Hardware Limitations:** True WIPS requires dedicated radio sensors that continuously monitor all Wi-Fi channels for
  threats without interrupting normal client traffic. Most home routers only have 2 or 3 radios dedicated entirely to serving
  your devices.
- **Software Complexity:** WIPS platforms (such as those from Cisco, Aruba, or Arista) rely on centralized cloud controllers
  or heavy management servers to analyze security policies and isolate threats.

---

### How to Implement WIPS-Like Security at Home

To protect your home network from wireless intrusions, rogue access points, and unauthorized devices, you can use the
following approaches:

#### 1. Upgrade to Prosumer / Enterprise-Lite Ecosystems (The Closest Option)

If you want hardware-level monitoring similar to a WIPS, you can replace or supplement your ISP router with a prosumer
ecosystem (such as **Ubiquiti UniFi** or **TP-Link Omada**).

- **How it works:** These systems feature dedicated management interfaces where you can enable features like "Rogue Access
  Point Detection."
- **What it does:** The system scans the surrounding airspace, alerts you if a neighboring device or malicious actor is
  mimicking your Wi-Fi name (an evil twin attack), and lets you monitor every client connecting to your airspace.

#### 2. Utilize Built-In Router Security Features

Most modern home routers (including newer mesh systems from Google, Eero, or ASUS) include built-in security features that
mimic intrusion prevention on the network layer:

- **Access Control / MAC Filtering:** Manually approve every device that joins your Wi-Fi, blocking any unknown MAC addresses
  instantly.
- **AI-Driven Threat Defense:** Many modern app-managed routers include built-in security suites (like ASUS AiProtection or
  HomeCare) that scan incoming traffic for malicious payloads, botnet communication, and intrusion attempts.
- **Isolation Mode / Guest Networks:** Keep smart home gadgets or untrusted visitors on a separate Guest VLAN so they cannot
  access your primary devices even if compromised.

#### 3. Implement Network-Layer Intrusion Detection (IDS/IPS)

If you want deep packet inspection and intrusion prevention, you can add a dedicated security appliance _behind_ your
modem/router:

- **Raspberry Pi or Old PC with Open-Source Software:** Install software like **Suricata** or **Snort** on a local device or
  a custom router running **pfSense** or **OPNsense**.
- **Commercial Security Gateways:** Devices like Firewalla or a UniFi Cloud Gateway sit between your modem and router,
  actively inspecting traffic, blocking intrusions, and alerting your phone to suspicious connection attempts in real-time.

---

### Best Practices for Securing a Home Wi-Fi Network

If you cannot run a full WIPS, you can secure your home airspace by following these critical steps:

- **Use WPA3 Encryption:** Ensure your router is set to use **WPA3-Personal** (or at minimum WPA2-AES) to prevent attackers
  from easily capturing and cracking your Wi-Fi handshake.
- **Disable WPS (Wi-Fi Protected Setup):** WPS buttons and PINs are historically vulnerable to brute-force attacks. Turn this
  feature off in your router settings.
- **Regularly Audit Connected Devices:** Check your router’s companion mobile app periodically to verify every device
  currently connected to your network.
