## Denial of Service (DoS) in Wireless Networks

A **Denial of Service (DoS) attack** in wireless networks aims to disrupt, degrade, or completely block legitimate users from
accessing a wireless medium (Wi-Fi). Because wireless networks broadcast data over shared airwaves using radio frequencies,
they are inherently more vulnerable to interference and interception than wired networks.

---

### How Wireless DoS Differs from Wired DoS

- **Medium Accessibility:** Anyone within range of the Access Point (AP) can listen to or transmit radio signals.
- **Shared Spectrum:** The airwaves are a shared medium; if an attacker floods the frequency, all communication stops.
- **Control Frames:** Unlike wired networks where traffic is encapsulated in packets, wireless networks rely heavily on
  unauthenticated management and control frames (like deauthentication requests), which are prime targets for exploitation.

---

### Common Types of Wireless DoS Attacks

#### 1. Deauthentication Attack (Deauth Attack)

- **Mechanism:** The attacker sends forged **Deauthentication frames** to a client device, pretending to be the Access Point,
  or vice versa (sending them to the AP pretending to be the client).
- **Impact:** This forces the client to disconnect from the Wi-Fi network. If automated and continuous, the attacker can
  completely prevent the victim from reconnecting.
- **Vulnerability:** In standard Wi-Fi (802.11), management frames like deauthentication are traditionally **unencrypted**,
  making them easy to spoof. _(Note: The newer 802.11w / Management Frame Protection standard helps mitigate this)._

#### 2. Radio Frequency (RF) Jamming

- **Mechanism:** An attacker uses a hardware device (jammer) to transmit continuous noise or signals on the same frequency
  channel (e.g., 2.4 GHz or 5 GHz bands) used by the Wi-Fi network.
- **Impact:** This overpowers legitimate signals, raising the noise floor and causing massive packet corruption and dropped
  connections.
- **Types:** \* _Constant Jammer:_ Continuously emits radio signals.
- _Reactive Jammer:_ Only transmits when it detects network traffic.

#### 3. Association/Authentication Flooding

- **Mechanism:** The attacker overwhelms the Access Point by sending a massive number of fake connection requests
  (Association Requests) using randomized MAC addresses.
- **Impact:** The AP exhausts its available memory and CPU resources trying to track and allocate sessions for these fake
  clients, preventing legitimate users from connecting.

#### 4. Clear-to-Send (CTS) / Request-to-Send (RTS) Flooding

- **Mechanism:** The 802.11 protocol uses RTS/CTS frames to manage channel access and prevent collisions. An attacker floods
  the network with forged CTS frames claiming the channel is busy.
- **Impact:** Legitimate clients stay silent, waiting for the channel to clear, effectively halting all network traffic.

---

### Mitigation and Defense Strategies

- **Upgrade to WPA3 and 802.11w:** \* **WPA3** and **Protected Management Frames (PMF)** encrypt wireless management frames,
  preventing attackers from successfully forging deauthentication or disassociation packets.
- **Deploy Wireless Intrusion Detection Systems (WIDS):** \* WIDS solutions monitor the airwaves for anomalous patterns, such
  as a sudden spike in deauthentication frames or unusual RF noise, and alert administrators.
- **Use Enterprise-Grade Hardware:** \* Modern enterprise access points have built-in mitigations against flooding attacks
  and can automatically channel-hop or isolate rogue interference.
- **Physical Security:** \* Secure AP locations to prevent physical tampering, localized jamming devices, or unauthorized
  wired plug-ins.
