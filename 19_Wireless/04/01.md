**WLAN Authentication** is the crucial security process of verifying the identity of a user or device attempting to connect
to a Wireless Local Area Network (Wi-Fi). It acts as a digital gatekeeper, ensuring that only authorized individuals or
devices gain access to network resources and data.

---

### Key Types of WLAN Authentication Standards

Over the years, wireless security protocols have evolved significantly to combat emerging cyber threats:

- **Open System Authentication:** Offers no security or credentials. Anyone within range can connect. It is typically used
  for public hot spots (though often paired with a captive portal).
- **WEP (Wired Equivalent Privacy):** The original standard from 1997. It uses a static shared key, but it is heavily
  outdated and easily compromised within seconds. **Avoid using it.**
- **WPA / WPA2 (Wi-Fi Protected Access):** \* **WPA2-PSK (Pre-Shared Key):** The standard for home and small office networks,
  relying on a single shared passphrase for all users.
- **WPA2-Enterprise:** Designed for businesses, requiring individual user credentials verified via an external backend server
  (like RADIUS).

- **WPA3:** The modern gold standard in wireless security. It provides enhanced protections against brute-force password
  cracking and adds individual data encryption even on open networks.

---

### Common Authentication Modes in Action

Depending on the environment, different workflows are used to clear access:

| Method                  | Best Used For                   | How It Works                                                                                                    |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Personal / PSK**      | Homes, small networks           | Every device uses the exact same Wi-Fi password to authenticate.                                                |
| **Enterprise (802.1X)** | Corporate offices, universities | Users log in with unique credentials (username/password or digital certificates) processed by a central server. |
| **Captive Portal**      | Hotels, cafes, airports         | Users connect to an open network first, then are redirected to a web page to agree to terms or log in.          |

---

> **Security Note:** Always opt for **WPA3** or at minimum **WPA2-PSK** with a strong, complex password for private networks
> to prevent unauthorized snooping and protect your personal data.
