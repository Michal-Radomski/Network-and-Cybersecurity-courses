Performing a thorough WLAN audit or penetration test requires specialized software and hardware capable of monitoring,
capturing, and interacting with radio frequency (RF) transmissions.

The industry-standard tools used by security professionals are categorized below by their primary functions.

---

## 1. Packet Sniffers and Wireless Analyzers (Layer 1 & 2 Mapping)

These tools allow security professionals to inspect raw wireless traffic, discover hidden networks, and analyze signal
quality.

- **Kismet:** A powerful open-source wireless network detector, sniffer, and intrusion detection system (IDS). It works
  passively, meaning it listens to traffic without transmitting packets, making it completely stealthy. It can identify
  hidden SSIDs, map out rogue access points, and even detect non-Wi-Fi signals like Bluetooth.
- **Wireshark:** The world's leading network protocol analyzer. When paired with a compatible wireless adapter in monitor
  mode, Wireshark captures individual frames (management, control, and data frames) to troubleshoot connection issues or
  inspect network traffic headers.

---

## 2. Wireless Auditing and Cracking Suites (Evaluation & Exploitation)

These toolsets are designed to assess the strength of wireless encryption and test how resilient a network is against
unauthorized access.

- **Aircrack-ng:** One of the most famous and comprehensive suites for assessing Wi-Fi security. It focuses on evaluating
  security by:
- **Monitoring:** Capturing wireless packets.
- **Attacking:** Launching de-authentication attacks to force clients to disconnect and reconnect (capturing the 4-way
  handshake in the process).
- **Testing:** Using dictionary and brute-force methods to test the strength of WPA/WPA2 pre-shared keys (PSKs).

- **Wifite2:** An automated wireless auditing tool built on top of tools like Aircrack-ng, Reaver, and Hashcat. It simplifies
  the auditing workflow by automatically scanning for targets, capturing handshakes or PMKIDs, and attempting to crack them
  with minimal manual input.
- **Airgeddon:** A multi-use shell script framework used for auditing wireless networks. It handles complex tasks like Evil
  Twin attacks, rogue access point creation, and captive portal testing through an interactive menu system.

---

## 3. RF Spectrum Analyzers and Site Survey Tools (Layer 1 Assessment)

These tools help evaluate the physical environment, map out coverage, and identify interference.

- **Acrylic Wi-Fi / Ekahau:** Professional site survey tools used to generate visual heatmaps of signal strength, noise
  levels, and channel overlap.
- **Hardware Spectrum Analyzers (e.g., MetaGeek Wi-Spy):** Physical hardware devices that plug into a computer to visualize
  radio frequency interference from non-Wi-Fi sources, such as microwave ovens, Bluetooth devices, and industrial equipment.

---

## Essential Hardware Requirements

Software alone isn't enough to audit a WLAN. Auditors also need specific hardware:

- **Compatible Wireless Network Interface Card (NIC):** The Wi-Fi card built into standard laptops rarely supports **Monitor
  Mode** (listening to all air traffic) and **Packet Injection** (sending custom frames). Auditors typically use external USB
  adapters containing specific chipsets (such as Atheros or certain Realtek chips) that support these advanced modes.
