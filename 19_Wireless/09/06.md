## What are WIDS and WIPS?

- **WIDS (Wireless Intrusion Detection System):** A system that monitors the radio spectrum for unauthorized access points,
  rogue devices, and suspicious wireless activity, alerting administrators when a security violation is detected.
- **WIPS (Wireless Intrusion Prevention System):** An extension of WIDS that not only detects threats but also takes active
  countermeasures to block or mitigate them automatically without requiring manual intervention.

---

## Key Differences Between WIDS and WIPS

| Feature                     | WIDS (Detection)                                                | WIPS (Prevention)                                                                     |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Primary Action**          | Monitors and **alerts** administrators to security events.      | Detects **and actively blocks** threats in real-time.                                 |
| **Response Type**           | Passive (logs events, sends emails or dashboard notifications). | Active (disrupts unauthorized connections, neutralizes rogue devices).                |
| **Risk of False Positives** | Low operational risk since it only reports issues.              | Higher potential impact if misconfigured (could accidentally block legitimate users). |

---

## Common Threats Detected and Blocked

- **Rogue Access Points:** Unauthorized access points plugged into the wired corporate network by employees, creating a
  backdoor for attackers.
- **Evil Twin / Spoofed APs:** Fake access points broadcasting the same SSID as the legitimate network to trick users into
  connecting and exposing their credentials.
- **Ad-Hoc Networks:** Peer-to-peer connections created by users within range of the corporate network, which bypass central
  security controls.
- **MAC Spoofing:** Attackers or unauthorized clients mimicking a legitimate device's MAC address to bypass access control
  lists (ACLs).
- **Denial of Service (DoS) Attacks:** Flooding the wireless medium with de-authentication frames to disconnect users from
  the network.

---

## How WIPS Countermeasures Work

When a WIPS identifies a severe threat (such as an Evil Twin or a rogue device trying to compromise the network), it can
deploy targeted countermeasures:

- **De-authentication Flooding (Counter-Attack):** The WIPS sends targeted de-authentication frames to force the rogue device
  or compromised client to disconnect from the unauthorized network. _(Note: This technique is strictly regulated and
  typically only used on enterprise networks owned by the organization)._
- **Switch Port Disabling:** Modern integrated WIPS architectures can communicate with enterprise network switches to
  automatically shut down the physical port where a rogue access point is plugged in.
