## What is WLAN Penetration Testing?

**WLAN Penetration Testing** is an authorized, simulated cyberattack against a wireless local area network. Unlike a standard
audit (which focuses heavily on configurations and compliance), a penetration test actively tries to exploit vulnerabilities
to determine if an attacker could bypass security controls, steal data, or gain unauthorized access to the network.

---

## The WLAN Penetration Testing Lifecycle

A typical wireless penetration test follows a structured methodology to ensure thorough coverage without causing unintended
disruption to business operations.

### 1. Scoping and Information Gathering (Reconnaissance)

- **Target Identification:** Define the target SSIDs, physical locations, and acceptable testing windows.
- **Passive Reconnaissance:** Without interacting with the network, testers monitor radio frequencies to capture beacon
  frames, identify target channels, map access point (AP) vendor types, and discover connected client MAC addresses.
- **Active Reconnaissance:** Probe the wireless environment to determine supported security protocols (WPA2, WPA3, Enterprise
  authentication) and check for hidden networks.

### 2. Threat Modeling and Vulnerability Analysis

- Analyze reconnaissance data to find potential attack vectors, such as legacy encryption, weak pre-shared keys, or
  misconfigured guest portals.
- Determine if the organization uses secure certificate-based authentication (like EAP-TLS) or vulnerable credentials.

### 3. Exploitation Phase

During this phase, testers attempt to exploit identified weaknesses:

- **Attacking Pre-Shared Keys (WPA/WPA2-PSK):** Capturing the 4-way handshake when a client connects to the network, then
  attempting to crack the passphrase offline using dictionary or brute-force attacks.
- **Rogue Access Point / Evil Twin Attacks:** Setting up an unauthorized access point that mimics the corporate Wi-Fi name to
  trick users or devices into connecting, potentially capturing credentials or relaying traffic.
- **Client-Targeted Attacks (De-authentication):** Sending forged de-authentication frames to force connected clients to drop
  their connection and automatically reconnect, which makes capturing handshakes or launching Evil Twin attacks easier.
- **Captive Portal and Guest Network Escapes:** Testing whether a guest network properly isolates users or if vulnerabilities
  in the captive portal allow unauthorized access to the internal network.

### 4. Post-Exploitation (Simulated Impact)

- Once access is gained, testers evaluate what an attacker could do next—such as moving laterally from the wireless network
  to the wired corporate network, accessing sensitive servers, or intercepting unencrypted traffic.

### 5. Reporting and Remediation

- Compiling a comprehensive report detailing successful attack paths, proof-of-concept evidence, and the business impact of
  each vulnerability.
- Providing clear, prioritized steps for remediation (e.g., upgrading to WPA3-Enterprise, implementing robust network
  segmentation, or deploying Wireless Intrusion Prevention Systems (WIPS)).

---

## Key Differences: WLAN Audit vs. Penetration Test

| Feature          | WLAN Audit                                                  | WLAN Penetration Test                                                        |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Primary Goal** | Check compliance, configuration health, and coverage.       | Actively exploit weaknesses to measure real-world risk.                      |
| **Methods**      | Scanning, reviewing settings, site surveys, and interviews. | Active exploitation, handshake cracking, and social engineering simulations. |
| **Outcome**      | A checklist of misconfigurations and policy gaps.           | Proof of concept showing how an attacker could breach the network.           |
