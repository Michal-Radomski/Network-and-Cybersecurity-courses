## What is a WLAN Audit?

A **Wireless Local Area Network (WLAN) Audit** is a systematic security assessment of a wireless network. Its primary goal is
to identify vulnerabilities, ensure proper configuration, verify compliance with security policies, and protect the network
from unauthorized access or cyber threats.

Wireless networks are inherently more vulnerable than wired networks because radio waves travel beyond physical walls, making
them accessible from outside a building if not properly secured.

---

## Key Objectives of a WLAN Audit

- **Discover Unauthorized Devices:** Identify rogue access points (APs), unauthorized routers, or shadow IT devices plugged
  into or broadcasting within the network infrastructure.
- **Evaluate Security Configurations:** Check encryption standards (e.g., ensuring WPA3 or WPA2-Enterprise is used instead of
  outdated protocols like WEP or WPA).
- **Assess Signal Leakage:** Measure wireless coverage boundaries to ensure signals do not bleed too far into public spaces
  or parking lots, which makes eavesdropping easier.
- **Test Access Controls:** Verify that guest networks are properly isolated from internal corporate or sensitive home
  networks.
- **Ensure Compliance:** Confirm that the network adheres to industry regulations and standards (such as PCI-DSS, HIPAA, or
  ISO 27001).

---

## Phases of a WLAN Audit

A comprehensive wireless audit typically follows several structured phases:

### 1. Preparation and Scope Definition

- Define the boundaries of the audit (e.g., specific office locations, floors, or SSID lists).
- Obtain necessary authorization and legal sign-offs (crucial to ensure penetration testing or scanning is not mistaken for a
  malicious attack).
- Review existing network architecture diagrams and security policies.

### 2. Discovery and Mapping

- **Site Survey:** Walk through the premises to map out physical access point locations and coverage areas.
- **Passive Scanning:** Listen to wireless traffic without transmitting data to identify legitimate and rogue access points,
  channels used, and signal strengths.
- **Active Scanning:** Probe networks to gather detailed information about connected clients and device capabilities.

### 3. Vulnerability Assessment and Testing

- **Encryption and Authentication Check:** Analyze how clients authenticate (e.g., checking for weak pre-shared keys, lack of
  multi-factor authentication, or misconfigured captive portals).
- **Firmware and Software Audit:** Check if access points and routers are running outdated, vulnerable firmware versions.
- **Rogue AP Detection:** Look for unauthorized devices attempting to mimic legitimate networks (Evil Twin attacks).

### 4. Reporting and Remediation

- Compile findings into a detailed report highlighting high, medium, and low risks.
- Provide actionable recommendations to patch vulnerabilities (e.g., updating firmware, changing default administrator
  passwords, strengthening encryption, or adjusting transmission power).

---

## Common WLAN Vulnerabilities Found During Audits

- **Weak Passwords:** Using easily guessable pre-shared keys (PSKs) for Wi-Fi access.
- **Default Credentials:** Leaving factory-set usernames and passwords on wireless access points or routers.
- **Outdated Protocols:** Utilizing legacy standards like WEP or WPA, which can be easily cracked.
- **Misconfigured Guest Networks:** Allowing guest users to access internal network resources due to a lack of proper VLAN
  segmentation.
- **Hidden SSIDs Misconception:** Relying on hiding the network name (SSID) as a security measure, which offers virtually no
  protection against modern scanners.
