In wireless network (WLAN) security and performance assessments, audits are frequently broken down based on the bottom two
layers of the OSI model: **Layer 1 (Physical Layer)** and **Layer 2 (Data Link Layer)**.

Because Wi-Fi relies entirely on radio waves and framing protocols transmitted through the air, these two layers form the
foundational bedrock of wireless security and functionality.

---

## 1. Layer 1 (Physical Layer) Audit

The **Layer 1 audit** focuses on the physical environment, hardware, and radio frequency (RF) medium through which wireless
signals travel.

### Core Focus Areas:

- **RF Coverage and Site Survey:** Analyzing signal strength, propagation, and dead zones to ensure physical infrastructure
  matches the intended design.
- **Signal Leakage:** Measuring how far the Wi-Fi signal extends beyond the physical boundaries of a building to prevent
  external eavesdropping.
- **Physical Interference (Non-Wi-Fi Interference):** Detecting sources of noise or interference that degrade performance or
  disrupt service, such as Bluetooth devices, microwave ovens, baby monitors, or industrial machinery.
- **Layer 1 Denial of Service (DoS):** Identifying malicious or accidental **RF jammers** that flood the airwaves with
  continuous transmission to block legitimate traffic.
- **Hardware Inspection:** Reviewing access point (AP) placement, physical mounting security, physical cabling integrity, and
  ensuring hardware is running current, supported firmware.

### Common Tools Used:

- **Spectrum Analyzers:** To visualize RF energy, noise floors, and non-Wi-Fi interference across 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, and 6 GHz
  bands.
- **Site Survey Software:** To map signal coverage and propagation in real-time.

---

## 2. Layer 2 (Data Link Layer) Audit

The **Layer 2 audit** focuses on the Media Access Control (MAC) sublayer and Logical Link Control (LLC). It evaluates how
data is framed, how devices authenticate and associate with access points, and how securely the air interface is managed.

### Core Focus Areas:

- **Device and Rogue Discovery:** Identifying unauthorized access points, hidden SSIDs, ad-hoc client connections, and
  "rogue" devices plugged into the wired network by employees or attackers.
- **Authentication and Encryption Evaluation:** Testing the strength of connection protocols. Auditors check whether outdated
  and easily compromised standards (like WEP or WPA/WPA2-PSK with weak passwords) are in use, or if modern secure standards
  (like WPA3 or WPA2/3-Enterprise) are properly configured.
- **Layer 2 Attacks:** Testing the network's resilience against common wireless attacks, such as:
- **De-authentication Floods:** Sending forged disconnect frames to force clients off the network.
- **Evil Twin / Rogue AP Attacks:** Fake access points designed to trick users into connecting and exposing credentials.

- **Client Isolation and Segmentation:** Verifying that guest users are properly isolated at the data link layer so they
  cannot communicate directly with internal corporate or home network devices.

### Common Tools Used:

- **Wireless Protocol Analyzers / Sniffers (e.g., Wireshark, Kismet):** To capture frames, analyze traffic headers, and
  inspect beacon frames.
- **Offensive Security/Penetration Testing Tools:** To safely test for vulnerabilities like weak pre-shared keys or
  vulnerability to de-authentication frames.
