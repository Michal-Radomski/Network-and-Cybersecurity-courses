### Evolution of WLAN Encryption Methods

As wireless local area networks (WLANs) evolved, securing radio frequency transmissions became critical. Because wireless
signals travel through the air, anyone within range can capture them. To prevent unauthorized eavesdropping and tampering,
several encryption and authentication standards have been developed over the years, steadily replacing broken or outdated
protocols.

---

### 1. WEP (Wired Equivalent Privacy) — 1997

- **Status:** Obsolete and completely insecure.
- **Overview:** WEP was the original encryption standard for IEEE 802.11 wireless networks. Its goal was to make wireless
  security match the physical safety of a wired network.
- **How it worked:** It used the **RC4 stream cipher** paired with a static shared secret key and a small 24-bit
  Initialization Vector (IV).
- **Why it failed:** Due to a small IV size, keys were frequently reused. Automated tools can crack WEP keys via statistical
  analysis in a matter of minutes.

---

### 2. WPA (Wi-Fi Protected Access) — 2003

- **Status:** Obsolete / Deprecated.
- **Overview:** Introduced as a temporary "stopgap" measure to secure existing hardware while a permanent standard was built.
- **Key Enhancements:** \* Implemented **TKIP (Temporal Key Integrity Protocol)**, which dynamically changed keys per packet
  to fix WEP's key-reuse flaw.
- Added Message Integrity Check (MIC) to prevent data tampering.

- **Why it failed:** TKIP still relied heavily on the flawed RC4 cipher framework, leaving it vulnerable to cryptographic
  attacks and key-recovery exploits over time.

---

### 3. WPA2 (Wi-Fi Protected Access 2) — 2004

- **Status:** Legacy standard (still widely deployed, but being phased out by WPA3).
- **Overview:** The long-term replacement for WEP and WPA, based on the full IEEE 802.11i standard. It became mandatory for
  Wi-Fi certified devices starting in 2006.
- **Key Enhancements:**
- Replaced RC4 with the robust **AES (Advanced Encryption Standard)** block cipher, offering military-grade security.
- Introduced **CCMP (Counter Mode with Cipher Block Chaining Message Authentication Code Protocol)** for data confidentiality
  and integrity.

- **Modes of Operation:**
- **WPA2-Personal (PSK):** Uses a pre-shared key (a standard password) for home and small networks.
- **WPA2-Enterprise (802.1X):** Uses a central authentication server (like RADIUS), giving each user unique credentials or
  certificates.

- **Vulnerabilities:** While strong, WPA2 is susceptible to the KRACK (Key Reinstallation Attack) vulnerability, which
  targets the 4-way handshake process to manipulate session keys.

---

### 4. WPA3 (Wi-Fi Protected Access 3) — 2018

- **Status:** Current modern standard.
- **Overview:** The latest generation of Wi-Fi security, designed to address the foundational weaknesses of WPA2.
- **Key Enhancements:**
- **SAE (Simultaneous Authentication of Equals):** Replaces the WPA2 4-way handshake with a secure handshake protocol that
  protects against offline dictionary attacks (meaning an attacker cannot guess your Wi-Fi password by capturing traffic and
  trying words on their own computer).
- **Forward Secrecy:** Even if an attacker manages to capture network traffic and later discovers the Wi-Fi password, they
  cannot decrypt past sessions they recorded.
- **Individual Data Encryption:** In open or public Wi-Fi networks, WPA3-Personal introduces opportunistic wireless
  encryption (OWE) to encrypt traffic between your device and the router individually, preventing casual eavesdropping on
  public hotspots.

---

### Comparison of WLAN Encryption Standards

| Standard | Cipher Used     | Integrity Protocol | Status           | Best Use / Notes                                                                  |
| -------- | --------------- | ------------------ | ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **WEP**  | RC4 (24-bit IV) | CRC-32             | Obsolete         | Easily cracked in minutes; do not use.                                            |
| **WPA**  | RC4 (TKIP)      | MIC                | Deprecated       | Temporary fix; vulnerable to cryptographic attacks.                               |
| **WPA2** | AES             | CCMP               | Legacy / Active  | Strong standard, but vulnerable to offline dictionary attacks if the PSK is weak. |
| **WPA3** | AES-GCMP-256    | SAE / CCMP         | Current / Modern | Maximum security, robust handshake protection, and forward secrecy.               |
