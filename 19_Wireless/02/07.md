### OSI Layer for WLAN

A Wireless Local Area Network (WLAN) primarily operates across the bottom two layers of the OSI model:

- **Layer 1 (Physical Layer):** Handles the transmission and reception of raw radio frequency (RF) signals through the air,
  converting digital data into physical carrier waves.
- **Layer 2 (Data Link Layer):** Divided into two sublayers:
- **Medium Access Control (MAC):** Manages device addressing (MAC addresses) and channel access mechanisms (such as CSMA/CA
  used in Wi-Fi to prevent collisions).
- **Logical Link Control (LLC):** Manages frame synchronization, flow control, and error checking above the physical medium.

---

### Key IEEE Standards and OSI Mapping

WLAN technology is defined by the **IEEE 802.11** family of standards (commonly known as Wi-Fi). Within these standards:

- **802.11 physical specifications** (like 802.11n, 802.11ac, or Wi-Fi 6/7 radio modifications) map directly to the
  **Physical Layer**.
- **802.11 MAC protocols, framing, encryption (like WEP, WPA2, WPA3), and authentication** operate at the **Data Link
  Layer**.
