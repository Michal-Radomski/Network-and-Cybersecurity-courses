### Government and Industry Regulations for WLAN

Wireless LANs (WLANs) must comply with applicable **government regulations, cybersecurity requirements, and industry
standards**. These requirements help protect wireless communications and prevent unauthorized access.

- **Government regulations:** Government organizations may have mandatory security requirements for wireless networks. For
  example, U.S. federal agencies use requirements based on **FISMA, NIST standards, and agency-specific policies**. NIST SP
  800-153 provides guidance for securing WLANs throughout their lifecycle. ([NIST][1])

- **IEEE 802.11 standards:** WLAN equipment should follow the **IEEE 802.11** family of standards. Modern security
  deployments should use strong authentication and encryption rather than obsolete technologies such as WEP. ([NIST Computer
  Security Resource Center][2])

- **WPA2/WPA3:** Organizations should use modern Wi-Fi security mechanisms such as **WPA2-Enterprise or WPA3-Enterprise**,
  with appropriate authentication and encryption. Enterprise networks commonly use **802.1X/EAP** for individual user/device
  authentication. ([U.S. General Services Administration][3])

- **Data-protection regulations:** Organizations handling sensitive information must also consider regulations applicable to
  that data. For example, healthcare organizations in the U.S. must implement appropriate administrative, physical, and
  technical safeguards under the **HIPAA Security Rule**. ([HHS.gov][4])

- **Industry compliance:** Depending on the organization, WLAN security may need to support requirements from frameworks or
  standards such as **PCI DSS** for payment-card environments and other sector-specific security standards.

- **Poland/EU:** In Poland, organizations can also use the **National Cybersecurity Standards (NSC)**, which provide
  cybersecurity recommendations based on standards such as NIST and are intended to support organizations operating within
  Poland's cybersecurity framework. ([gov.pl][5])

**In summary:** WLAN security policies should require approved encryption, strong authentication, secure configuration,
regular monitoring, vulnerability management, and compliance with all applicable legal and industry requirements.
