**Wireless Security Policies** are rules and controls used to protect wireless networks (Wi-Fi) from unauthorized access,
eavesdropping, attacks, and misuse.

Key components typically include:

1. **Authentication**
   - Use WPA3 or WPA2-Enterprise where possible.
   - Prefer 802.1X authentication with a RADIUS server for organizational networks.
   - Avoid shared passwords for large or sensitive environments.

2. **Encryption**
   - Require strong Wi-Fi encryption.
   - Prohibit outdated protocols such as WEP and legacy WPA/TKIP.
   - Use strong, unique credentials and cryptographic keys.

3. **Access Control**
   - Restrict network access to authorized users and devices.
   - Use network segmentation/VLANs to separate employees, guests, IoT devices, and critical systems.
   - Disable unnecessary wireless services.

4. **Guest Networks**
   - Provide a separate guest SSID.
   - Prevent guests from accessing internal corporate resources.
   - Apply bandwidth, authentication, and usage restrictions as appropriate.

5. **Device Security**
   - Keep access points, controllers, and client devices patched.
   - Change default administrator usernames/passwords.
   - Disable unused management interfaces and services.

6. **Monitoring and Logging**
   - Monitor for rogue access points, unauthorized clients, and suspicious activity.
   - Retain relevant authentication and security logs.
   - Investigate repeated authentication failures or unusual wireless activity.

7. **Physical Security**
   - Place wireless infrastructure in physically secure locations.
   - Protect network equipment from unauthorized tampering.
   - Consider wireless signal coverage when positioning access points.

8. **Incident Response**
   - Define procedures for compromised credentials, rogue access points, and suspected wireless attacks.
   - Have a process for disabling compromised accounts or devices.
   - Document and investigate security incidents.

9. **Acceptable Use**
   - Specify who may connect personal devices.
   - Define requirements for BYOD devices.
   - Prohibit unauthorized hotspots or personal access points on corporate networks.

10. **Regular Review**

- Periodically audit wireless configurations and access permissions.
- Remove unused SSIDs, accounts, and devices.
- Review the policy whenever major network or security changes occur.

A good wireless security policy should balance **confidentiality, integrity, availability, usability, and regulatory
requirements** while clearly assigning responsibilities to users and IT/security teams.
