A **WLAN password policy** defines how passwords used to protect a wireless network must be created, managed, and changed.

Key requirements include:

- **Strong passwords:** Use long, complex passwords containing a mix of letters, numbers, and symbols. For
  WPA2/WPA3-Personal, a passphrase of **at least 12–16 characters** is recommended.
- **Unique passwords:** Do not reuse the WLAN password for other systems or services.
- **Password protection:** Never share WLAN passwords publicly or transmit them through insecure channels.
- **Regular changes:** Change shared WLAN passwords periodically and immediately when they may have been compromised or when
  an authorized user with access leaves the organization.
- **Enterprise authentication:** For business WLANs, prefer **WPA2-Enterprise or WPA3-Enterprise with 802.1X/RADIUS**, so
  users authenticate individually rather than sharing one password.
- **Account protection:** Enforce appropriate lockout or rate-limiting controls against repeated authentication attempts
  where supported.
- **Default credentials:** Change all default administrator and wireless credentials before deploying access points.
- **Secure storage:** Store administrative credentials in an approved password manager or other secure credential-management
  system.
- **Guest access:** Use a separate guest WLAN with separate credentials and prevent guest users from accessing internal
  resources.

The goal is to prevent **unauthorized access, password guessing, credential theft, and lateral movement into the
organization's network**.
