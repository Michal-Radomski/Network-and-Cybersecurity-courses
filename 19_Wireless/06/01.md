## SOHO 802.11 Wireless Networks

**SOHO (Small Office/Home Office)** networks represent the standard wireless setup used in residential homes and small
business environments. Built on the **IEEE 802.11** suite of standards (commonly known as Wi-Fi), these networks bridge local
devices (laptops, smartphones, smart home gadgets) to the wider internet via a single residential gateway or wireless router.

Unlike massive enterprise systems that rely on complex hardware arrays, dedicated VLANs, and central RADIUS authentication
servers, SOHO 802.11 networks prioritize simplicity, cost-effectiveness, and plug-and-play setup.

## SOHO 802.11 Security Standards

Because SOHO networks are typically managed by everyday users rather than dedicated IT departments, their security models are
designed to be user-friendly while still blocking unauthorized access.

The security architecture of a SOHO Wi-Fi network primarily relies on personal-mode authentication frameworks:

- **WPA2-Personal (PSK):** The long-standing baseline for home networks. It uses a Pre-Shared Key (the Wi-Fi password)
  combined with the Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) to encrypt traffic. While secure against basic eavesdropping, it
  relies heavily on the strength of a single shared password.
- **WPA3-Personal (SAE):** The modern standard found in newer SOHO routers. It replaces traditional key exchanges with
  **Simultaneous Authentication of Equals (SAE)**, protecting networks against offline brute-force attacks even if the user
  chooses a weak, easy-to-remember password.
- **WPA2/WPA3 Transition Mode:** A common hybrid setting on SOHO routers that allows modern devices to connect using secure
  WPA3 protocols while still accommodating older smart home gadgets that only support WPA2.

## Key Security Best Practices for SOHO Networks

Securing a small office or home network goes beyond just choosing the right Wi-Fi protocol. Standard best practices include:

- **Strong Administrative Passwords:** Changing the default factory login credentials of the wireless router to prevent
  unauthorized changes to firewall rules or port forwarding.
- **Network Segmentation:** Utilizing **Guest Networks** built into modern SOHO routers to isolate untrusted visitor devices
  or Internet of Things (IoT) hardware from sensitive computers containing personal files or work documents.
- **Disabling Legacy Protocols:** Ensuring that outdated, highly vulnerable legacy standards like WEP or unsecured open
  networks are completely turned off.
