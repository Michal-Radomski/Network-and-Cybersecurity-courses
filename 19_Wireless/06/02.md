When you connect your laptop to a Wi-Fi router, a series of invisible steps happens in just a fraction of a second. This
process bridges your device to your home network (SOHO 802.11) and grants you access to the internet.

Here is how the connection process looks step-by-step:

---

### Phase 1: Discovery (Finding the Network)

- **Broadcasting Beacons:** Your router constantly blasts out tiny packets called **Beacon Frames** that advertise its
  presence, containing the network name (SSID), supported speeds, and security requirements.
- **Scanning:** When you open your laptop's Wi-Fi menu, it either listens for these beacons or sends out a **Probe Request**
  asking, _"Is my home network (MySSID) nearby?"_
- **The Response:** The router replies with a **Probe Response**, confirming it is there and telling your laptop what
  security protocol it uses (like WPA2 or WPA3).

---

### Phase 2: Authentication (Proving Who You Are)

- **Open Authentication:** Before any heavy security takes place, 802.11 requires a basic "open authentication" exchange.
  This is essentially a polite handshake where the laptop asks to join, and the router says "go ahead"—though at this point,
  no encrypted data can pass through yet.

---

### Phase 3: Association (Linking Up)

- **Association Request:** Your laptop sends an Association Request to the router containing its hardware address (MAC
  address) and capabilities.
- **Association Response:** The router accepts the request, assigns a logical association ID, and officially links your
  laptop to its wireless interface. _At this stage, your laptop is associated, but it still doesn't have the keys to talk
  securely._

---

### Phase 4: Security & Key Exchange (The 4-Way Handshake)

- **The Handshake:** If the network is secured (which all modern networks are), the router and laptop execute the **4-Way
  Handshake**.
- Using your Wi-Fi password (or pre-shared key) combined with unique random numbers (nonces), both devices secretly prove
  they know the password without ever shouting it over the air.
- Once verified, they generate temporary session keys (**PTK**) so that all your web browsing, streaming, and chatting data
  is completely encrypted.

---

### Phase 5: IP Assignment (Getting an Address)

- **DHCP (Dynamic Host Configuration Protocol):** Now that your laptop is securely connected, it needs an address to talk to
  other devices and the internet.
- Your laptop shouts out a **DHCP Discover** request: _"Is there a router out there that can give me an IP address?"_
- Your router responds with an **IP Offer** (e.g., `192.168.1.50`), a subnet mask, and a gateway address. Your laptop accepts
  it, and you are officially online!
