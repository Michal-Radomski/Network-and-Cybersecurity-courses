The **FT (Fast Transition) Key Hierarchy** is a cryptographic architecture defined in the **IEEE 802.11r** standard. It is
specifically designed to allow wireless clients to roam rapidly and securely between different access points (APs) in an
enterprise environment without having to repeat a heavy, time-consuming authentication process (like a full 802.1X/EAP
exchange).

---

## How the FT Key Hierarchy Works

Instead of generating session encryption keys directly from a central master key every time a device moves to a new AP, the
802.11r standard breaks down key derivation into a structured, hierarchical chain. This allows keys for target APs to be
pre-calculated or securely distributed in advance.

The top-to-bottom hierarchy flows through the following stages:

```text
MSK (Master Session Key) / PSK (Pre-Shared Key)
  └── PMK (Pairwise Master Key)
        └── PMK-R0 (Root Key)
              └── PMK-R1 (R1 Master Key)
                    └── PTK (Pairwise Transient Key)

```

---

## Breakdown of Key Levels

### 1. MSK or PSK (The Root)

- **What it is:** The very top source of trust. In an enterprise network, this is the **MSK (Master Session Key)** generated
  after a successful 802.1X authentication with a RADIUS server. In a home or pre-shared key setup, it comes from the **PSK
  (Pre-Shared Key)**.
- **Role:** Acts as the absolute foundational secret from which all other keys derive.

### 2. PMK-R0 (Pairwise Master Key R0)

- **What it is:** The first derived master key, bound to a specific **Mobility Domain (MD)** (a group of APs that share
  fast-roaming capabilities) and the client's MAC address.
- **Role:** Created jointly by the client and an **R0 Key Holder (R0KH)** (often a wireless controller or a primary AP). It
  localizes security domains so keys aren't exposed across the entire network infrastructure.

### 3. PMK-R1 (Pairwise Master Key R1)

- **What it is:** A secondary master key derived from the PMK-R0, specific to a single individual target Access Point (the
  **R1 Key Holder** or **R1KH**).
- **Role:** Provides unique isolation for each specific AP the client might roam to. If one AP is compromised, the keys for
  other APs remain secure.

### 4. PTK (Pairwise Transient Key)

- **What it is:** The final operational session key used to encrypt actual data traffic sent over the air between the client
  and the target AP.
- **Role:** Derived during the fast roaming (FT) handshake using the PMK-R1, nonces, and MAC addresses.

---

## Why the FT Key Hierarchy Matters

- **Eliminates Latency:** By pre-deriving the PMK-R1 and PTK keys before the client actually finishes its physical move to a
  new AP, the 802.11r protocol shrinks the roaming handshake down to just a few frames (often under 50 milliseconds).
- **Uninterrupted Applications:** This ultra-fast handoff prevents data loss, making it ideal for latency-sensitive
  applications like Voice over Wi-Fi (VoWiFi) and live video streaming.
- **Maintains Enterprise Security:** Even though authentication is bypassed during roaming, the cryptographic chain
  guarantees that every target AP uses unique, dynamically generated encryption keys.
