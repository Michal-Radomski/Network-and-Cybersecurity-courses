## What is Layer 3 (L3) Roaming?

**Layer 3 Roaming** is a networking process that allows a wireless client (like a smartphone, laptop, or VoIP phone) to move
from one Access Point (AP) to another across **different subnets or VLANs** without losing its connection or changing its IP
address.

Unlike Layer 2 (L2) roaming—where a device moves between APs on the same subnet—an L3 roam crosses a network routing
boundary.

---

## How L3 Roaming Works

When a device roams across different subnets, it normally would need to request a new IP address via DHCP. This process
causes delays that drop active sessions (such as voice or video calls). L3 roaming solves this through tunneling:

- **The Anchor Controller/AP:** The original device location acts as the "anchor." It keeps track of the client's session.
- **The Foreign Controller/AP:** The new location where the client has roamed to.
- **The Tunnel:** A secure tunnel (such as a CAPWAP or auto-tunnel) is established between the foreign AP/controller and the
  anchor point.

Because of this tunnel, all network traffic for the roaming device continues to be routed through its original subnet,
allowing it to **keep its original IP address** transparently.

---

## Key Benefits

- **Session Persistence:** Active applications like VoIP calls or VPN connections do not drop when users walk across large
  enterprise campuses.
- **Seamless Mobility:** Users can move freely between different departments, buildings, or floors assigned to separate VLANs
  without needing to manually reconnect to the Wi-Fi.
