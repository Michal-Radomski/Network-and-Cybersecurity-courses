### What is 802.11 Roaming?

**802.11 roaming** refers to the process where a wireless client device (like a smartphone, tablet, or laptop) moves between
different Access Points (APs) within the same wireless network (Extended Service Set or ESS) without losing its connection.

Think of it like walking through a large corporate office or school: as you walk away from one Wi-Fi router and closer to
another, your device needs to hand off its connection smoothly so your video call or file download doesn't drop.

---

### How the Roaming Process Works

The roaming decision is made almost entirely by the **client device**, not the router. The process generally follows three
main stages:

1. **Monitoring:** The client continuously measures the signal strength (RSSI) and link quality of its current Access Point.
2. **Scanning:** When the signal drops below a certain threshold, the client begins searching for alternative APs
   broadcasting the same network name (SSID).
3. **Reassociation and Authentication:** Once a better AP is found, the client breaks ties with the old AP and authenticates
   with the new one.

---

### The Problem: Roaming Latency

In a basic setup, switching between APs requires a fresh round of security handshakes and authentication checks.

- On enterprise networks using heavy security, this process can take hundreds of milliseconds.
- For real-time applications like VoIP (voice calls) or video streaming, even a brief delay can cause audio stutter, lag, or
  dropped packets.

To fix these performance lags, the IEEE introduced standard amendments to speed up the handoff:

---

### Key Enhancements for Seamless Roaming

| Standard    | Name                            | What It Does                                                                                                                                                      |
| ----------- | ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **802.11k** | **Radio Resource Measurement**  | Provides the client with a **neighbor report** (a list of nearby APs and their channels) so the device doesn't have to waste time blindly scanning every channel. |
| **802.11r** | **Fast BSS Transition (FT)**    | Allows encryption keys to be pre-calculated and shared across APs in advance, drastically speeding up the authentication handshake when moving to a new AP.       |
| **802.11v** | **Wireless Network Management** | Allows the network infrastructure to guide client devices, suggesting better APs or load-balancing connections to prevent overcrowding.                           |

---

### Common Roaming Challenges

- **"Sticky" Clients:** Some devices stubbornly hold onto a weak connection from a distant AP rather than roaming to a
  closer, stronger one. This is usually a client-side behavior issue.
- **Improper AP Overlap:** If access points are placed too far apart, users experience dead zones; if they are placed too
  close with high transmission power, it causes channel interference and erratic roaming behavior.
