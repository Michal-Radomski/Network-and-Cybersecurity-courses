A **CAPWAP tunnel** (Control and Provisioning of Wireless Access Points) is a standard, secure networking protocol used to
manage enterprise wireless networks. It creates a logical connection between a **Wireless LAN Controller (WLC)** and multiple
**Access Points (APs)**, allowing a network administrator to centrally manage dozens or thousands of access points from a
single location.

---

### How a CAPWAP Tunnel Works

When using CAPWAP, access points are typically "lightweight" (sometimes called "dumb" APs), meaning they do not make
autonomous decisions about routing, security handshakes, or configurations. Instead, they forward wireless traffic back to
the central controller.

The CAPWAP protocol splits communication into two distinct channels:

- **Control Channel:** \* **Purpose:** Used for management, configuration updates, firmware pushes, and monitoring.
- **Port:** Uses **UDP port 5246**.
- **Security:** Always encrypted using **DTLS** (Datagram Transport Layer Security) to prevent unauthorized control or
  tampering.

- **Data Channel:** \* **Purpose:** Encapsulates and forwards actual user data frames coming from wireless clients
  (smartphones, laptops) through the tunnel to the WLC.
- **Port:** Uses **UDP port 5247**.
- **Security:** Can be encrypted using DTLS, though it is sometimes left unencrypted depending on performance needs and
  licensing.

---

### Key Benefits of CAPWAP

- **Centralized Management:** Administrators can configure security policies, SSIDs, and updates on the WLC once, and it
  automatically pushes those settings out to all connected APs.
- **Seamless Roaming:** Because the WLC handles client sessions and traffic routing, devices can roam between different APs
  across a building without dropping their connection or needing to re-authenticate from scratch.
- **Enhanced Security:** By wrapping wireless traffic inside encrypted UDP packets, data is protected even if it travels
  across an untrusted local network switch or segment.
