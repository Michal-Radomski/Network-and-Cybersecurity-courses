When configuring **802.11r Fast Transition (FT)** roaming for enterprise Wi-Fi networks, you must choose between two methods
for how a client device negotiates its security keys with a new Access Point (AP): **Over-the-Air (OTA)** or **Over-the-DS
(Distribution System)**.

Both methods accomplish the same ultimate goal—minimizing roaming latency for voice and video applications—but they handle
the communication path differently.

---

## 1. Over-the-Air (OTA) Fast Transition

In **Over-the-Air** roaming, the client device communicates **directly** with the target (new) Access Point via wireless
radio frames before it actually drops its connection to the current AP.

- **How it works:** 1. While still connected to AP-1, the client sends an FT Authentication Request directly over the air to
  AP-2.

2. AP-2 responds directly back through the air to the client.
3. Once the security handshake is verified, the client immediately switches its association and completes the roam.

- **Pros:** \* Faster transition times in most environments.
- Widely supported by almost all enterprise client devices and AP manufacturers.

- **Cons:** \* The client has to briefly interrupt its data flow with the current AP to transmit and listen on the target
  AP's radio channel.

---

## 2. Over-the-DS (Distribution System) Fast Transition

In **Over-the-DS** roaming, the client device communicates with the target AP **indirectly through its current AP and the
wired network infrastructure** (such as a wireless controller or switches).

- **How it works:**

1. While connected to AP-1, the client sends an FT Action Request frame to AP-1.
2. AP-1 encapsulates this request and forwards it across the wired Distribution System (DS) or controller to AP-2.
3. AP-2 processes it and sends the response back through the wired network to AP-1, which hands it back to the client.
4. Because the cryptographic handshake is completed over the wires _before_ moving, the client only switches radio channels
   when it is 100% ready to finalize the roam.

- **Pros:** \* The client does not need to pause its current connection to probe or talk to remote radio channels ahead of
  time, reducing radio-frequency (RF) overhead.
- **Cons:** \* Relies heavily on a stable, low-latency wired network backbone.
- Some older or specific client devices have poor compatibility with Over-the-DS implementations, leading to roaming drops.

---

## Key Comparison Summary

| Feature                  | Over-the-Air (OTA)                                                                  | Over-the-DS (OTDS)                                                                                          |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Communication Path**   | Directly between client and target AP through the air.                              | Routed from client $\rightarrow$ current AP $\rightarrow$ wired network/controller $\rightarrow$ target AP. |
| **Channel Interruption** | Client briefly tunes to target AP's channel during handshake.                       | Client stays on its current channel until the final switch.                                                 |
| **Network Dependency**   | Relies on clean wireless signaling between AP coverage overlaps.                    | Relies on healthy wired backbone and controller infrastructure.                                             |
| **Industry Adoption**    | **Most common**; default recommendation for most vendor setups (like Cisco/Meraki). | Used selectively for specific client device requirements or specialized designs.                            |
