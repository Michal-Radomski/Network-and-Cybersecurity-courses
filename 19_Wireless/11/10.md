In security, **NAC** usually means **Network Access Control**.

It controls **which devices and users are allowed to connect to a network and what level of access they receive**.

### Example

Imagine an office Wi-Fi:

1. Your laptop tries to connect.
2. NAC checks things such as:
   - Who you are
   - Whether your device is authorized
   - Whether security requirements are met (e.g., antivirus, updates)

3. NAC decides what happens:
   - ✅ **Compliant device** → full internal network access
   - ⚠️ **Unknown/non-compliant device** → restricted or guest network
   - ❌ **Unauthorized device** → connection blocked

### ACL vs. NAC

|                 | **ACL**                              | **NAC**                                               |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Main purpose    | Control access to a resource/traffic | Control access to a network                           |
| Focus           | Permissions and traffic rules        | Users and devices                                     |
| Example         | Allow `10.0.0.5` to access port 443  | Allow an employee's laptop onto the corporate network |
| Common location | Firewall, router, OS, cloud          | Network infrastructure / access-control system        |

**Easy way to remember:** **ACL = “What are you allowed to access?”** **NAC = “Are you allowed onto the network?”**
