### What is RBAC?

**RBAC (Role-Based Access Control)** is a popular method used to restrict network and system access based on the specific
roles of individual users within an organization.

Instead of assigning permissions directly to every single user—which becomes messy and impossible to manage as a company
grows—administrators assign permissions to **roles** (such as Manager, Developer, or HR Specialist), and then assign users to
those roles.

---

### How RBAC Works

1. **Users:** The individuals who need access to system resources (employees, contractors, etc.).
2. **Roles:** Job functions or operational titles created within the organization (e.g., _Database Administrator_, _Helpdesk
   Technician_).
3. **Permissions:** The actions a user can perform on specific resources (e.g., _Read_, _Write_, _Delete_, _Execute_).
4. **Assignment:** \* Permissions are bundled together and assigned to a role.

- Users are placed into one or more roles based on their job requirements.

---

### Core Principles of RBAC

- **Least Privilege:** Users are given only the bare minimum permissions necessary to perform their job duties, reducing
  security risks if an account is compromised.
- **Separation of Duties (SoD):** Prevents fraud or errors by ensuring critical tasks require multiple people across
  different roles (e.g., the person who creates a financial transaction cannot also be the one who approves it).

---

### Key Benefits

- **Simplified Administration:** When an employee changes roles or leaves the company, an IT administrator doesn't need to
  manually update dozens of individual file permissions. They simply remove the user from one role and add them to another.
- **Enhanced Security:** Minimizes the attack surface by ensuring sensitive company resources, source code, or financial data
  are only exposed to authorized job titles.
- **Compliance Ready:** Helps organizations easily meet regulatory requirements (like HIPAA, GDPR, or PCI-DSS) by providing a
  clear audit trail of who has access to what data.
