Setting up a **Controller-Based Access Point (AP)** architecture at home is entirely possible and a great way to experience
enterprise-grade Wi-Fi management. However, because traditional enterprise controllers (like heavy hardware boxes) are
expensive and complex, home networks usually achieve this using **software-defined or cloud-managed ecosystems**.

---

### Popular Hardware Ecosystems for Home Use

To build a controller-based setup at home, you need an ecosystem where the APs are managed by a central software or cloud
brain:

- **Ubiquiti UniFi:** Uses a software controller (the UniFi Network Application) that can run on a local PC, a Raspberry Pi,
  or a dedicated device called a UniFi Cloud Key.
- **TP-Link Omada:** Similar to UniFi, it uses an Omada software controller or a hardware controller (OC200/OC300) to manage
  Omada-series EAP access points.
- **Aruba Instant On:** A cloud-managed controller approach where you manage your access points via a mobile app or web
  browser, with the controller hosted in the cloud by Aruba.

---

### Step-by-Step Implementation Guide (Software-Controller Approach)

#### Step 1: Procure the Right Equipment

- **A Router:** Your standard home internet gateway/router (from your ISP or your own router like pfSense or an ASUS/TP-Link
  consumer router) to provide DHCP and internet access.
- **A PoE Switch or PoE Injectors:** Controller-based APs are typically powered via Ethernet (**Power over Ethernet**). You
  will need a PoE-enabled network switch or individual PoE injectors to plug into the APs.
- **Controller-Based Access Points:** Buy 1 or more APs from an ecosystem like UniFi or Omada.
- **Controller Host:** A device to run the management software (if not using a cloud or hardware controller).

#### Step 2: Set Up the Central Controller

1. Download and install the controller software (e.g., UniFi Network Application) onto a computer, server, or a dedicated
   piece of hardware like a Raspberry Pi. Alternatively, plug in a dedicated hardware controller (like the TP-Link OC200) to
   your network switch.
2. Open the controller dashboard via a web browser (usually via a local IP address or `https://localhost:8443`).
3. Follow the initial setup wizard to create an administrator account, name your site, and configure basic network settings.

#### Step 3: Connect and Adopt the Access Points

1. **Physical Wiring:** Plug an Ethernet cable from your home router or switch into a PoE injector/PoE switch port, and run
   another cable from there into the LAN/PoE port of your Access Point.
2. **IP Assignment:** The AP will boot up and receive a local IP address from your home router's DHCP server.
3. **Adoption:** Log into your central controller dashboard. Under the "Devices" tab, you will see the new AP listed with a
   status of **"Pending"** or **"Managed by Other"**. Click **Adopt** to bring the AP under the control of your central
   dashboard.

#### Step 4: Configure Wireless Networks (SSIDs)

1. Inside the controller dashboard, navigate to **Settings > Wireless Networks** (or Wi-Fi).
2. Create your Wi-Fi name (SSID) and password.
3. The controller will automatically push these settings down to your adopted access point(s), which will immediately begin
   broadcasting the Wi-Fi signal.

#### Step 5: Advanced Home Configuration (Optional)

Once your controller-based setup is running, you can easily configure advanced features right from the central dashboard:

- **Guest Wi-Fi & Captive Portal:** Set up a separate guest network with a landing page or terms-of-service agreement.
- **VLANs:** If you have a smart switch and a router that supports VLANs, you can segment your network (e.g., isolating IoT
  smart home devices from your main computer).
- **Roaming Tuning:** Adjust radio transmit power and channel widths to ensure seamless handoffs as you walk around your
  house.
