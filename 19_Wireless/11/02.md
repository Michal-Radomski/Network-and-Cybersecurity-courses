### What is a Controller-Based Access Point?

A **controller-based access point** (often called a "thin" AP) is a wireless access point that relies entirely on a
centralized hardware or virtual **WLAN Controller (WLC)** to manage its operations, security, configurations, and traffic
flow.

Unlike standalone (fat) access points that make their own decisions, controller-based APs act primarily as radio antennas,
forwarding data and control instructions back to the central controller.

---

### How Controller-Based APs Work

1. **Split-MAC Architecture:** The workload is divided between the AP and the controller:

- **The AP** handles real-time, low-level physical functions like transmitting radio frequency (RF) signals, encrypting
  wireless frames, and handling basic client acknowledgments.
- **The Controller** handles the heavy lifting, including authentication, configuration management, roaming decisions,
  security policies, and radio resource management (channel/power allocation).

2. **Tunneling (CAPWAP):** Communication between the AP and the controller is encapsulated using protocols like **CAPWAP**
   (Control and Provisioning of Wireless Access Points). All client traffic is often tunneled back through the controller so
   that corporate firewalls can inspect it uniformly.

---

### Key Benefits

- **Seamless Roaming:** Because the controller manages the session state for all clients, users can move (roam) from one
  access point to another across a large building without dropping their connection or having to re-authenticate.
- **Centralized Management:** Administrators can update firmware, push configuration changes, and monitor health metrics for
  hundreds or thousands of APs simultaneously from a single dashboard.
- **Automatic Radio Management:** The controller continuously monitors the airspace for interference and automatically
  adjusts channel selections and power levels for all connected APs to optimize performance.
- **Unified Security:** Security policies, firewalls, and intrusion detection systems are applied centrally, ensuring
  consistent protection across the entire wireless network.
