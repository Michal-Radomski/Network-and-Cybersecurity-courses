While **Wireless**, **WLAN**, and **Wi-Fi** are often used interchangeably in everyday conversation, they actually refer to
different layers of networking concepts—ranging from a massive umbrella term down to a specific brand technology.

---

### Quick Comparison Table

| Term         | What is it?                                                                     | Scope / Range                                                      | Example                                                             |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Wireless** | The overarching concept of sending data using waves instead of cables.          | Anywhere from inches to millions of miles                          | Bluetooth, GPS, Cellular data, Wi-Fi, Satellite                     |
| **WLAN**     | A localized network configuration that connects devices without physical wires. | Local area (a home, office, school, or single building)            | A network linking your laptops and printers at home via radio waves |
| **Wi-Fi**    | A specific set of technical standards used to build and operate a WLAN.         | Short-to-medium range (typically within 50–100 meters of a router) | Connecting your phone to your home wireless router                  |

---

### Breaking Down Each Term

#### 1. Wireless (The Broadest Category)

**Wireless** is an umbrella term for _any_ technology that transmits data without physical cables, using radio frequencies,
infrared, or Bluetooth.

- If it cuts the cord, it is wireless.
- This includes things you use every day that have nothing to do with home internet, such as Bluetooth headphones, garage
  door openers, TV remote controls, and mobile cellular networks (4G/5G).

#### 2. WLAN (Wireless Local Area Network)

A **WLAN** is a specific type of network. A "Local Area Network" (LAN) connects computers and devices together in a small
geographic area (like inside a house or office). Adding the "Wireless" part means they connect via radio waves instead of
Ethernet cables.

- **The Concept:** Think of WLAN as the _blueprint_ or the category of network setup.
- While almost all modern WLANs use Wi-Fi, a WLAN can technically be built using other communication protocols (like older
  infrared systems or proprietary industrial radio systems).

#### 3. Wi-Fi (The Specific Technology)

**Wi-Fi** is a trademarked name and a specific set of technical standards (specifically the IEEE 802.11 family) used to make
WLANs work.

- **The Implementation:** If WLAN is the blueprint of a wireless room, Wi-Fi is the specific brand of furniture used to fill
  it.
- When your phone says it is connected to a "Wi-Fi network," it means you are using Wi-Fi technology to access a WLAN, which
  ultimately links you to the broader internet via your router.

---

### How They Fit Together

To put it simply:

> **Wi-Fi** is a type of **WLAN**, and a **WLAN** is a type of **Wireless** technology.
