**IEEE 802.11** is the technical set of standards that defines **Wi-Fi**—the technology used for wireless local area
networking (WLAN). Maintained by the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE), these standards dictate how
wireless routers and devices communicate using radio frequencies.

---

## Evolution of Key Wi-Fi Generations

To make the technical names easier to understand, the Wi-Fi Alliance introduced consumer-friendly names (like Wi-Fi 5, Wi-Fi
6, Wi-Fi 7) starting in 2018.

| Standard     | Wi-Fi Name   | Year Released | Radio Frequency       | Max Data Rate (Theoretical) |
| ------------ | ------------ | ------------- | --------------------- | --------------------------- |
| **802.11b**  | Wi-Fi 1      | 1999          | 2.4 GHz               | 11 Mbps                     |
| **802.11a**  | Wi-Fi 2      | 1999          | 5 GHz                 | 54 Mbps                     |
| **802.11g**  | Wi-Fi 3      | 2003          | 2.4 GHz               | 54 Mbps                     |
| **802.11n**  | Wi-Fi 4      | 2009          | 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz        | Up to 600 Mbps              |
| **802.11ac** | Wi-Fi 5      | 2013          | 5 GHz                 | Up to 3.5 Gbps              |
| **802.11ax** | Wi-Fi 6 / 6E | 2019 / 2021   | 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, 6 GHz | Up to 9.6 Gbps              |
| **802.11be** | Wi-Fi 7      | 2024          | 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, 6 GHz | Up to 46 Gbps               |

## Key Breakthrough Technologies

Over the years, 802.11 updates introduced key innovations to handle more devices and higher speeds:

- **MIMO (Multiple Input, Multiple Output):** Introduced in **802.11n**, this technology allows routers to use multiple
  antennas simultaneously to transmit and receive data, improving speed and signal reliability.
- **MU-MIMO (Multi-User MIMO):** Expanded in **802.11ac** and **802.11ax**, enabling a router to communicate with multiple
  devices at the exact same time instead of switching between them sequentially.
- **OFDMA (Orthogonal Frequency-Division Multiple Access):** Introduced in **802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6)**, this breaks Wi-Fi channels
  into smaller sub-channels so one transmission can deliver data to several devices at once, significantly cutting down
  latency in crowded environments.
- **6 GHz Band Support:** Introduced with **Wi-Fi 6E** and refined in **Wi-Fi 7**, opening up a brand-new, uncrowded
  frequency band for ultra-fast, low-latency connections.
