In network architecture (such as Cisco's classic 3-tier hierarchical network design), the **Access**, **Distribution**, and
**Core** layers work together to keep traffic organized, fast, and secure.

When applied to **wireless networking**, these layers define how wireless devices (like smartphones, laptops, and Wi-Fi
Access Points) connect back to the main network infrastructure.

---

## 1. Access Layer (The Entry Point)

The **Access Layer** is the edge of the network where end-user devices connect to the system.

- **Wireless Role:** This is where **Wi-Fi Access Points (APs)** live. End-user wireless clients (phones, laptops, IoT
  devices) connect over the air to these APs.
- **Key Functions:**
- Wireless authentication (WPA2/WPA3, 802.1X enterprise logins).
- Port security and initial Quality of Service (QoS) tagging.
- Powering wireless access points using Power over Ethernet (PoE).

- **Summary:** _Where devices join the network._

---

## 2. Distribution Layer (The Policy & Routing Engine)

The **Distribution Layer** sits in the middle and aggregates data coming from multiple access-layer switches and APs. It acts
as the bridge between the access edge and the core.

- **Wireless Role:** In centralized wireless networks, **Wireless LAN Controllers (WLCs)** or distribution switches operate
  here. They manage AP configurations, handle seamless roaming between APs, and route wireless traffic.
- **Key Functions:**
- Routing between different VLANs (Virtual Local Area Networks).
- Enforcing security policies, firewalls, and Access Control Lists (ACLs).
- Managing traffic filtering and bandwidth limits before sending data deeper into the network.

- **Summary:** _Where traffic is controlled, filtered, and routed._

---

## 3. Core Layer (The High-Speed Backbone)

The **Core Layer** is the high-speed backbone of the entire network. Its primary job is to move massive volumes of data as
fast and reliably as possible without slowing things down.

- **Wireless Role:** The core doesn't process individual Wi-Fi security rules or manage APs directly. Instead, it carries the
  aggregated wireless data from the distribution layer to external networks, servers, data centers, or the Internet.
- **Key Functions:**
- Ultra-fast switching and routing with minimal latency.
- Providing high redundancy so the network stays up even if a link fails.
- No heavy packet inspection or complex filtering (to maximize speed).

- **Summary:** _The high-speed highway carrying data across the network._

---

### Comparison Overview

| Layer            | Primary Hardware                          | Key Wireless/Network Role                                    |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Access**       | Wi-Fi Access Points, Edge Switches        | Connects wireless devices to the network                     |
| **Distribution** | Wireless Controllers, Multilayer Switches | Applies security policies, handles roaming, and routes VLANs |
| **Core**         | High-capacity Backbone Switches/Routers   | Transporting data at maximum speed with zero bottlenecks     |
