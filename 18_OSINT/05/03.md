**Maps OSINT** (Open-Source Intelligence mapping or **Geolocation OSINT**) is the practice of using publicly available maps,
satellite imagery, geospatial databases, and visual clues to determine, verify, or analyze the geographic location of an
event, object, or person.

It is widely used by investigative journalists, human rights organizations, cybersecurity professionals, search-and-rescue
teams, and researchers to verify information and uncover facts from open sources.

---

### Key Techniques Used in Maps OSINT

- **Metadata Analysis:** Inspecting digital files (like photos or videos) for EXIF data, which can occasionally contain
  embedded GPS coordinates, timestamps, or camera settings.
- **Visual Geolocation:** Analyzing environmental features in images or videos—such as unique architectural styles, mountain
  ranges, vegetation, language on signs, road markings, and utility poles—to narrow down a location.
- **Shadow and Sun Angle Analysis:** Using the position of shadows in media to calculate the time of day and rough
  latitude/longitude of where or when a piece of media was captured.
- **Triangulation:** Utilizing signposts, distances, or multiple fixed landmarks visible in media to draw overlapping radii
  on a map until the exact intersection (target location) is found.

---

### Common Tools and Platforms

- **Mapping & 3D Imagery:** \* _Google Maps / Google Earth / Google Street View:_ Essential for ground-level verification,
  historical imagery, and 3D terrain exploration.
- _OpenStreetMap (OSM) & Overpass Turbo:_ Open-source mapping platforms allowing users to query specific geographic features
  (like power plants, benches, or building types) using tags.

- **Specialized Analysis Tools:**
- _SunCalc:_ Calculates sun positioning, movement, and shadow lengths for any given date, time, and location.
- _GeoHints:_ A reference database that helps investigators identify countries based on regional clues like bollards, license
  plates, road lines, and electrical infrastructure.
- _ExifTool:_ A command-line tool used to read, write, and edit image and video metadata.
