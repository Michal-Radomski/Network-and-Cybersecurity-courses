**Mapillary.com** is a crowdsourced, street-level imagery platform—often described as a community-driven alternative to
Google Street View. Instead of relying on a dedicated fleet of company cars, Mapillary allows everyday people, organizations,
and governments from all over the world to capture and upload photos and videos of streets, parks, and trails.

Key aspects of the platform include:

- **Crowdsourced Coverage:** Anyone can use a smartphone, action camera (like a GoPro), or dashcam to take photos while
  walking, biking, or driving, and then upload them to the platform. This allows remote or less-traveled areas to be mapped
  where traditional services might not go.
- **Computer Vision and Automation:** Mapillary uses artificial intelligence and computer vision to process the uploaded
  images. It automatically stitches photos together into a smooth, navigable street-level view, extracts map features (such
  as traffic signs and road furniture), and automatically blurs faces and license plates to protect privacy.
- **Integration with OpenStreetMap:** The platform is heavily used by mapmakers and urban planners. The imagery and data
  extracted from Mapillary integrate directly with **OpenStreetMap** to help people update maps, track construction, or
  manage city infrastructure.
- **Free to Use:** Founded in 2013 and acquired by Meta (Facebook) in 2020, the platform and its data are free to access,
  view, and use for hobbyists, researchers, developers, and municipalities.
