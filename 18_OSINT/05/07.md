Both **CMS** and **LMS** are types of software platforms used to manage digital information, but they are built for entirely
different purposes.

---

### 1. CMS (Content Management System)

A CMS is a software application that allows users to build, manage, and publish digital content on a website without needing
advanced coding knowledge.

- **Primary Purpose:** Organizing, editing, and displaying general web content (like text, images, and videos).
- **Key Features:** Drag-and-page builders, user permission controls, SEO (Search Engine Optimization) tools, and media
  libraries.
- **Common Examples:** WordPress, Wix, Squarespace, and Shopify.
- **Typical Use Case:** Running a public blog, a business website, an online portfolio, or an e-commerce store.

---

### 2. LMS (Learning Management System)

An LMS is a software platform specifically designed to create, deliver, track, and report on educational courses and training
programs.

- **Primary Purpose:** Managing structured learning experiences and tracking learner performance.
- **Key Features:** Course assignment pathways, quizzes, grading tools, progress tracking, and certifications.
- **Common Examples:** Canvas, Blackboard, Moodle, and Google Classroom.
- **Typical Use Case:** Schools and universities delivering online classes, or companies training employees on safety and job
  skills.

---

### Key Differences at a Glance

| Feature                | CMS (Content Management System)              | LMS (Learning Management System)                      |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Target Audience**    | General web visitors, readers, or customers  | Students, employees, or registered learners           |
| **Core Focus**         | Public-facing content delivery and branding  | Education, training, and skill development            |
| **Tracking & Testing** | Tracks website traffic, views, and clicks    | Tracks quiz scores, course completion, and compliance |
| **Structure**          | Flat structure (pages, blog posts, articles) | Hierarchical structure (modules, lessons, courses)    |
