**Image OSINT** (Open-Source Intelligence), sometimes called IMINT (Imagery Intelligence), is the practice of extracting,
analyzing, and verifying information from digital photographs, screenshots, and video frames using publicly available sources
and tools.

Investigators, journalists, security researchers, and fact-checkers use image OSINT to answer key questions: _Where was this
taken? When was it taken? Is it authentic, or has it been edited?_

---

### Core Components of Image OSINT

An image analysis typically relies on three main categories of signals:

#### 1. Metadata Analysis (EXIF Data)

Digital cameras and smartphones often embed hidden data directly into image files known as **EXIF (Exchangeable Image File
Format)** data.

- **What it reveals:** Camera make and model, shutter speed, ISO, timestamps, and sometimes even exact GPS coordinates.
- **The catch:** Most major social media platforms and messaging apps automatically strip metadata when you upload a photo to
  protect user privacy. Therefore, analysts prefer to examine the original, uncompressed file (`.jpg`, `.png`, etc.) whenever
  possible.

#### 2. Reverse Image Searching

Instead of looking at _what_ is in a picture, reverse image search engines look for _where else_ that picture appears on the
internet.

- **How it works:** You upload an image to find older duplicates, higher-resolution copies, or the original source.
- **Common tools:** Google Lens, TinEye, Yandex (often effective for facial matching or finding different angles), and Bing
  Visual Search.
- **Why it matters:** It helps debunk fake news or out-of-context claims by proving a photo circulating today was actually
  taken years ago during a completely different event.

#### 3. Visual Clues and Geolocation

When metadata is missing and reverse image searches fail, analysts rely on environmental details within the photo to pinpoint
where it was captured:

- **Signage and Text:** Storefronts, license plates, road signs, and even graffiti text (which can be translated using
  Optical Character Recognition, or OCR) provide immediate geographic clues.
- **Architecture and Infrastructure:** Unique building designs, power pole configurations, bridge styles, and street
  furniture can narrow a location down to a specific city or neighborhood.
- **Nature and Weather:** Vegetation types, mountains, and coastline shapes offer regional context.
- **Shadow and Sun Analysis:** By looking at the length and angle of shadows, analysts can use tools like **SunCalc** to
  determine the approximate time of day or verify if a photo's stated timestamp matches the actual position of the sun.

#### 4. Image Forensics

Detecting whether an image has been manipulated, doctored, or generated by AI.

- **Techniques:** Tools like **Error Level Analysis (ELA)** inspect compression artifacts to see if parts of an image were
  digitally altered or pasted in from another photo.

---

### Common Use Cases

- **Journalism & Fact-Checking:** Verifying whether user-submitted footage from a conflict zone or natural disaster is real
  and current.
- **Cybersecurity & Threat Intelligence:** Analyzing photos shared online that might accidentally leak sensitive corporate or
  operational infrastructure in the background.
- **Human Rights Investigations:** Documenting evidence of human rights abuses by geolocating specific buildings or events.
