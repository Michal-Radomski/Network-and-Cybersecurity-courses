**People OSINT** (Open Source Intelligence on individuals) is the practice of collecting, analyzing, and correlating publicly
available information about a specific person from open sources.

Unlike private investigations that rely on confidential informants or wiretaps, OSINT relies entirely on data that is legally
accessible to the general public.

---

### Key Sources of People OSINT

Investigators, cybersecurity professionals, and researchers typically gather information from several main categories:

- **Social Media Profiles:** Public posts, photos, tagged locations, friend lists, and bios across platforms like LinkedIn,
  Instagram, X (formerly Twitter), Facebook, and TikTok.
- **Public Records:** Government and municipal databases, such as property ownership records, marriage and birth registries,
  court filings, business registrations, and voter registration rolls (where legally public).
- **Professional and Academic Footprints:** Resumes, academic publications, conference speaker bios, and corporate directory
  listings.
- **Data Breaches and Leaks:** Historical databases containing leaked email addresses, usernames, and passwords (often used
  defensively to see if an identity has been compromised).
- **Digital Infrastructure:** Associated domain names, personal websites, blog posts, and forum activity tied to a specific
  username or handle across the web.

---

### Common Use Cases

- **Cybersecurity and Privacy:** Ethical hackers and security awareness professionals use OSINT to understand what personal
  information is exposed online so they can help individuals secure their digital footprint.
- **Background Verification:** Journalists, recruiters, and private investigators use it to verify professional backgrounds,
  check credentials, or vet sources.
- **Digital Forensics and Law Enforcement:** Law enforcement agencies use OSINT within legal frameworks to locate missing
  persons, investigate fraud, or track cybercriminals.

---

### Privacy and Ethics

While People OSINT uses public data, it sits in a sensitive area regarding personal privacy. Unauthorized or aggressive data
collection used to stalk, harass, or dox (publish private information maliciously) individuals is unethical and often
illegal. Many privacy advocates emphasize practicing "opsec" (operations security) and digital hygiene—such as using strong
privacy settings, unique passwords, and minimizing personal data shared online—to reduce one's digital exposure.
